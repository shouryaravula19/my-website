Why I Invest in Bricks, Not Just Stocks

Real Estate: Cash Flow You Can Touch

Of all the asset classes I've studied or worked with, real estate is the one I keep coming back to. A big part of that is stability. Unlike many private investments — where capital gets locked up for years before an investor sees a single dollar back — real estate can generate consistent cash flow almost immediately, in the form of rent. That distinction matters more than it might seem: an investor holding a rental property isn't just betting on the property's value going up someday, they're also getting paid along the way.

Real estate is also a tangible asset. You can walk through it, point to it on a map, and understand exactly what you own. That's different from owning a slice of a fund whose underlying holdings you may never actually see. This tangibility makes the risk easier to reason about, which is part of why real estate tends to behave differently from more abstract, paper-only investments during periods of market stress.

My time at AEW Capital and Bain Capital reinforced this from the institutional side. Even at a much larger scale than a single rental unit, the core logic holds: income is the foundation, and appreciation is the upside on top of it, not the whole story.

Why Real Estate Holds Up as an Asset Class

  • Consistent cash flow. Rental income arrives regularly, rather than being locked away until an eventual exit.
  • Tangible collateral. The asset itself has intrinsic use and value, independent of market sentiment.
  • Inflation resistance. Rents and property values have historically adjusted upward alongside inflation.
  • Diversification. Real estate returns don't move in lockstep with public equities or bonds.
  • Leverage potential. Financing can amplify returns in a way that's harder to access in many other asset classes.
